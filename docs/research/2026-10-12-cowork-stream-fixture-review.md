**Required correction: install cleanup ownership immediately after spawn.** `run_stream_fixture.py:52–64` starts the direct child, writes metadata, receives/transfers the peer acknowledgement, starts the oracle, and only then enters `capture_owned_child`. The sole nearby `finally` closes the control socket; there is no surrounding cleanup for the child or peer. In particular, after line 57 sends `A`, the worker may proceed through its thread probe to `krun_start_enter` (`stream_fixture_worker.c:120–128`). A `RuntimeError` from `PeerOracle.begin()`/`Thread.start()`, a setup error, or an interruption before capture supervision then abandons an authorized-to-boot child without a finite kill/wait or a verified exit record. `block_capture.py:47–48` also starts reader threads before its own cleanup `try` at line 60. Its later cleanup does not cover a reader-start failure. The worker's independent 120-second alarm is useful defense in depth, but does not provide parent reaping or justify the unconditional owned-cleanup claim.

Put the entire post-`Popen` lifecycle under an outer `try/finally` that handles ordinary exceptions and cancellation, closes both control endpoints and any transferred peer according to ownership, stops/joins only an actually started oracle, and finitely kills/reaps only the owned direct child unless exit is already verified. Keep uncertain exits explicit and continue to skip disk/raw audits while their owners may still be running. Add no-VM fault injection covering an error after ACK but before capture, partial reader-thread startup, and interruption; demonstrate no leaked child/peer and finite waits. Do not use a process-name kill or kill a discovered PID.

**Required timing correction: make the advertised frame deadline apply to every loop iteration.** `stream_fixture_init.c:92–101` measures a frame start, but positive reads/writes take the `continue` at line 95 before checking elapsed time. Repeated positive short operations can therefore exceed the advertised ten-second frame deadline. Check an absolute monotonic deadline on every iteration, including the positive-progress path, with nanosecond-correct comparison. The outer worker alarm still bounds the whole experiment; this finding is about the stricter per-frame contract, not an unbounded guest-memory allocation.

**Required claim correction: the initial RX pause does not itself force host backpressure.** The guest sleeps two seconds before RX (`stream_fixture_init.c:88–89`), while the host refuses reads for three seconds and sends nothing until it has validated guest bytes (`stream_fixture_oracle.py:63–64,74–84`). Thus initial host input normally starts after that RX pause. Either describe the two-second delay only as initial pacing and retain the measured `WouldBlock` requirement, or change the bounded schedule so the claimed initial stall is deliberately exercised. The 250 ms per-frame RX pacing and small peer send buffer can still produce real host `WouldBlock`; that remains an observation to require from the actual run, not a result of the initial pause alone. A readiness stall is not a blocked OS syscall.
